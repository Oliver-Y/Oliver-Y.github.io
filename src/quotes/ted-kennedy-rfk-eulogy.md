Two days after Robert F. Kennedy was assassinated, his brother Ted delivered
this eulogy at St. Patrick's Cathedral in New York City. He's such a
powerful, commanding orator that embodies American idealism in the 60s.
Every time I re-listen, I just feel imbued with the vitality of what it means
to lead an American life.

> "Love is not an easy feeling to put into words. Nor is loyalty, or trust,
> or joy. But he was all of these. He loved life completely and he lived it
> intensely."

> "The answer is to rely on youth -- not a time of life but a state of mind,
> a temper of the will, a quality of imagination, a predominance of courage
> over timidity, of the appetite for adventure over the love of ease."

> "Some men see things as they are and say why. I dream things that never
> were and say why not."

> "It is the shaping impulse of America that neither fate nor nature nor the
> irresistible tides of history, but the work of our own hands, matched to
> reason and principle, that will determine our destiny. There is pride in
> that, even arrogance, but there is also experience and truth. In any
> event, it is the only way we can live."

Yet what always gets me is the end. After this rousing rhetoric, you can
hear the quiver in the voice as he begins to talk about his brother again. That resolute, powerful, resounding voice
quivering. It gives me goosebumps everytime. We're reminded that Tedd Kennedy is human just like the rest of us;
we're reminded of our common heritage, our common humanity, and our common
responsibility to uplift those around us stricken by
injustice, grief, and oppression.
